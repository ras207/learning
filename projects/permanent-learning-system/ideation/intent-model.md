## 1. Trigger or motivation

- **[stated]** Finishing courses and consuming content is not the outcome wanted. The user wants to *"continuously develop genuinely useful capabilities over years, rather than simply completing courses or consuming content."*
- **[inference]** The trigger is dissatisfaction with how poorly conventional learning turns into real capability. The seed does not say why this matters now, or what personal or professional situation is behind it (WI-003).

## 2. Current intent

What the user wants to create or explore:

- **[stated]** A *permanent, personal* learning system that works for years and ideally for life.
- **[reframed]** A way to keep knowing **what capability the user is aiming for, where they currently stand, and what the highest-value next learning step is**. The seed's wording for this is "model those capabilities and my evolving level of mastery" and "decide what I should work on next".
- **[stated]** Learning decisions are driven by **evidence** from learning sessions, projects, assessments and real-world performance.
- **[stated]** AI acts as adaptive tutor, curriculum manager and learning orchestrator. It designs sessions, challenges the user, finds weaknesses, connects domains and adapts the programme.
- **[stated]** Learning should move over time toward **real projects and consequential activity**.
- **[stated]** The system should get better over time at understanding the user and at choosing high-value opportunities.

## 3. Desired change

- **[stated]** The user moves from broad ambitions to **demonstrable real-world capability** in economics, technology, AI, business and other interests.
- **[stated]** A persistent body of knowledge, evidence, software, research, creative work and other artefacts builds up and is **valuable in its own right**, not only as a record of learning.
- **[stated]** The user's own reasoning, understanding and judgement grow stronger rather than being handed to AI.
- **[inference]** Success is judged by what the user can do and has produced, not by courses finished or credentials earned.

## 4. Relevant people

- **Primary learner and beneficiary. [stated]** The user, as a single individual ("personal", "me").
- **Other beneficiaries. [inference, unconfirmed]** People who might use or value the artefacts, such as collaborators, employers, clients or readers. The seed implies them through "valuable in its own right" and "demonstrable", but names nobody.
- **Critical stakeholders. [unknown]** None expressed.
- Whether anyone besides the user should ever use the system is not raised in the seed. That question belongs to ambition expansion (Gate 2). It is not an ambiguity in the current intent.

## 6. Solution commitments

- **AI as the active tutor, curriculum manager and orchestrator.** This is intentional: it is central to the seed. How much the AI directs versus advises is still open (A-001, WI-004).
- **An explicit model of capabilities and mastery.** Treated as a need (section 2, [reframed]), not a fixed mechanism. It stays open to alternatives at Gate 4.
- **A persistent artefact body.** This is intentional as an outcome (B6). Its form is left to later stages.
- Nothing in the seed commits to specific software, tools, platforms, schedules or data structures. None are assumed here.

## 8. Provisional assumptions

- **A-001.** B1 is about *cognition*: the user must do the thinking, so the capability is real. It does not stop the AI from leading curriculum and sequencing, as the seed asks explicitly. *Moderate risk.* If it is wrong, the operating model moves from AI-led to user-led with AI advice.
- **A-002.** The current intent is for one learner, the user. *Low risk.* The seed says so directly. Broadening is left to Gate 2.
- **A-003.** "Useful capability" is judged by real-world application and demonstrable output rather than by credentials. *Low risk.* It follows B2 and B6.
