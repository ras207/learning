# Vision: Permanent Learning System

Iteration 1 · produced by `synthesize-vision` · 2026-09-27

Sources: intent model, ambition map, problem model, alternative set, direction comparison and focused challenge in this folder; human decisions D-001 to D-005; gate evaluations G1-E1 to G5-E1.

## 1. Vision statement

A permanent, private learning system for one person: an economist who wants to spend years building a rare combination of deep economics, the ability to build and validate AI-driven analysis, and the skills to turn that into independent policy advice. The AI keeps a map of the user's capabilities and the evidence for each. It uses that map to choose, every session, what to build, maintain or test. Most learning happens through real projects that the user owns. The outcome is capability the user can demonstrate and that remains current for decades, with the reasoning and judgement always remaining the user's own.

## 2. Problem or opportunity

**What happens today.** The user has about seven fragmented hours a week: short slots around a commute and longer blocks at weekends. The learning that fits those hours is not interactive, does not keep up with fast-moving fields, and is not tailored to the user's strengths and weaknesses. Six months of learning about AI have been unstructured.

**Why this is inadequate.** Courses produce familiarity and completion, not capability. General AI chat is interactive but remembers nothing between sessions, does no sequencing, and easily does the thinking for the learner. Learning on the job gives real stakes for economics and advice, but the AI tools at work are extremely limited, so the AI-systems half of the trajectory has no outlet there. No course or programme covers the intersection the user is aiming at. Over a ten-year horizon, three deeper risks sit on top of these symptoms: skills decay, effort fades, and time spent organising learning crowds out learning itself.

**Who experiences it.** The user, whose targets are as follows. Within about one year: run agentic workflows they have built, refresh economic theory and econometrics, and begin to understand good software. Within about three years: build and validate agentic systems for robust economic analysis, converse credibly with academic economists and senior officials, and sell insight. In ten years and beyond: be a respected advisor on key policy issues across economics, financial markets and technology.

**Why it is worth addressing.** The value of these capabilities comes from combining them, and no external curriculum assembles that combination. Turning scarce, fragmented time into integrated, current, demonstrable capability is the difference between the user's intended career and business and continued, unstructured exposure.

## 3. People and stakeholders

### Primary users

The user, and only the user. They are an economist leading a small team that gives independent advice to government, hold a master's in economics, have some coding (R, some Python) and early AI experience, and have no business experience. Where later design trade-offs arise, the user's learning takes priority over everything else, including the system's own sophistication.

### Other beneficiaries

The user's team and government clients benefit indirectly as the user's analytical and AI capability grows. Future clients of the user's policy-advice business benefit later. Neither group uses the system.

### Critical stakeholders

- **The user's employer.** Its confidentiality rules limit what work material can ever enter the system.
- **Cloud AI providers.** They process session content under the user's chosen privacy position, and the system depends on their capability without being tied to any one of them.

## 4. Desired change

| Today | If the vision succeeds |
|---|---|
| Learning is content consumed in spare moments | Learning is practice and real projects, chosen for their value to the user's targets |
| Nothing remembers where the user stands | A durable, user-owned map of capabilities, each backed by evidence, is kept current |
| Material lags fast-moving fields | What is worth learning is reassessed as AI and policy debates move |
| Economics, AI and advisory skills develop separately, if at all | Projects at their intersection build them together |
| Skills fade unnoticed (the econometrics refresh) | Maintenance is scheduled, and neglected areas are visible |
| AI output improves while the user's reasoning may not | Capability counts only when the user shows the reasoning themselves |
| Progress is felt, not shown | A growing, private body of work demonstrates what the user can do |

## 5. Chosen direction and rationale

**Direction.** Learning happens mainly through real, private projects where economics, AI and policy meet, for example building and validating AI-assisted analysis of public policy questions using public data. The AI maintains a user-owned graph of capabilities and the evidence for each. Each session, it decides which part of the graph to **build** (usually through project work), **maintain** (refresh what is fading) or **test** (check whether a claimed capability holds). Foundations such as theory, econometrics and software practice are brought in when projects expose the need, and are kept alive by maintenance. Evidence of capability comes from AI assessment and the user's self-assessment. Other people's judgement is not a required source (D-005). The durable record is held in files the user controls, and the parts needed for a session are shown to a cloud AI (P2, D-004). The AI directs sequencing (D-001), and the user learns while building the system without running its construction as one of the system's own projects (D-002).

**Behavioural boundaries that define the direction.**

- The AI that assesses capability is kept separate from the AI that tutors and helps build. The assessor sees the capability claim and the user's own work, questions the user's reasoning directly, and where practical is a different model.
- AI-assisted output alone never counts as evidence that the user can do something.

**Why this direction.** Real projects are the only option that builds the integration the targets demand, and they produce demonstrable work from the start. The capability graph supplies what projects alone lack: sequencing, maintenance, and a record that outlives any tool. The following alternatives were considered and set aside:

- **An AI tutor working mainly through designed exercises.** This risks recreating the "content, not capability" failure.
- **An assembled set of existing products.** This gives up an owned, cross-domain picture of the learner.
- **Learning anchored on mentors and peers.** This depends on other people's availability.
- **Splitting work and personal learning.** This defers integration.

The agent had recommended adding periodic human judgement as evidence. The user chose not to, as the more realistic option, and accepted the weaker external credibility that follows.

## 6. Guiding principles

1. **The thinking stays the user's.** When help and learning conflict, choose the version in which the user reasons. Capability is evidenced only when the user demonstrates unaided reasoning, for example by explaining, defending or adapting the work.
2. **Prefer evidence the world can check.** Where possible, favour evidence that reality scores (code that runs, a published result reproduced, an estimate that holds on new data, a forecast scored against outcomes) over a model's opinion. Compare self-assessment with independent assessment so that over- and under-confidence become visible.
3. **Learning time comes before system time.** Start minimal and add to the system only when a limitation blocks learning. Building the system counts as learning only where it builds target capability.
4. **Balance across the trajectory is visible.** The graph shows when an area, such as economics, is starved by more absorbing technical work.
5. **The record is the user's and outlives every tool.** It is portable, private by default, and independent of any single AI provider or product. Sharing is chosen case by case.
6. **Choose what to learn with the frontier in view.** What is worth a human learning is reassessed as AI capability and the user's fields move, without chasing trends.

Principles 1 (the unaided-reasoning test) and 2 were proposed by the agent during the pre-synthesis challenge, within the user's decisions (WI-016). They are the user's to keep or reject when approving this vision.

## 7. Boundaries and non-goals

- **Capability, not results.** The system does not pursue income, ventures, roles or impact as goals. Projects are means and evidence (D-003).
- **Capability, not reputation.** The system evidences what the user can do. It does not measure or build how others regard them, which remains outside the system.
- **One learner.** It does not serve other people, cohorts or a product. Productisation is deferred until the system has demonstrably worked for the user over a sustained period, or until the user chooses to make it a venture (WI-009).
- **No credentials.** It is not a certification or verification service for third parties.
- **Private, not public.** Learning in public is not the engine. Sharing is an individual choice.
- **No work material.** Confidential work material stays out of the system unless the employer's rules allow it. Projects use public material. The user's own notes on how work went may count as self-reported evidence.
- **The AI does not do the work for the user.** Any arrangement in which the user mainly supervises AI output is out of scope.
- **Not an AI-hosted learner profile.** The detailed record is not held by a learning or AI provider.

## 8. Critical assumptions

| Assumption | Believed but not yet established | If false |
|---|---|---|
| **A-008** A cloud AI given the user-held record each session can tutor, assess and sequence well enough over months | Untested; test cheaply and early | The record and projects remain; existing tools can substitute for weak parts |
| **A-010** Separating the assessor from the tutor, and questioning the user's unaided reasoning, makes AI and self-assessment credible evidence | Plausible. Models from different providers may share blind spots | The graph overstates capability; world-checkable evidence becomes essential |
| **A-005** Commute slots suit maintain and test, and weekend blocks suit build | Inferred from the user's time pattern | Project work stalls; the balance between build and maintain must shift |
| **A-003** Useful capability is judged by demonstration, not credentials, even though the ten-year aim also depends on reputation | Accepted as a limit of the system, not resolved | Capability may not convert into standing; this is addressed outside the system |
| **Meta-work stays in proportion (WI-006)** | Mitigated by the AI keeping the graph and by principle 3 | Building and tending the system consumes the hours meant for learning |

## 9. Success looks like

**Outcome success**

- **Within about a year.** The user runs agentic workflows they built and can explain them, applies refreshed econometrics to real questions, and can reason about software quality. Each of these is evidenced in the graph by unaided demonstration.
- **Within about three years.** The user builds and validates AI-assisted economic analysis and can defend its robustness under hostile questioning. The private body of work shows this.
- **Throughout.** The user's own reasoning improves, not just their output. Capabilities stay current and do not quietly decay. Effort is sustained on about seven hours a week without the system itself becoming the hobby.

**Essential capability success**

- The graph reflects what the user can actually do. Its judgements hold up against world-checkable outcomes and the user's experience at work.
- Every session, the next step, whether build, maintain or test, is chosen well enough that time is not wasted on the wrong things.
- The record survives a change of AI provider or tool with nothing lost.

## 10. Unresolved questions

All are **non-blocking**. They can be resolved in design, research or early use.

- **Does record-based tutoring work well enough (A-008)?** To be answered by an early trial.
- **How independent must the assessor be, and which world-checkable evidence is practical in each domain (A-010)?** To be answered in design and early use.
- **Which existing tools, if any, should be components (WI-014)?** Not researched. This matters for design, not for direction.
- **How to keep meta-work in proportion in practice (WI-006)?** To be observed in the first months.
- **Whether the user later wants people's judgement as evidence.** This would revisit part of D-005 if the reputation gap proves costly.

There are no blocking questions.
